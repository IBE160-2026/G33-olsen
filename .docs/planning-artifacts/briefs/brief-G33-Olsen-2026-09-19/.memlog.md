- (event) User supplied a student brief template PDF, course presentation, Markdown brief template, and project-proposal PDF as references; extracted delivery cues: a roughly two-page product-brief.md covering what, why, who, and how.
- (decision) Candidate direction selected: a simplified AI-supported MRP II/MRP planning application. User works alone, is new to procurement, and wants a project between simple and challenging; scope should be a focused vertical slice rather than all six MRP II modules.
- (decision) Primary user: procurement planner/buyer with selected production-planning visibility. Primary outcomes: avoid stockouts while reducing excess inventory. Domain: fictional dairy manufacturer, inspired by user familiarity with milk production but using no real company data.
- (decision) Product catalogue preferred: multiple dairy products—three yogurt flavours, milk with multiple fat percentages, butter, and sour cream. User wants AI to both explain plans and help with forecasts/scenarios. Project format remains undecided pending course requirements and preference for the easiest viable option.
- (decision) Core user journey approved: user chooses a planned production week, sees ingredient and packaging requirements derived from product BOMs, and receives purchase recommendations. The project is a browser-based dashboard with a simple login and fictional dairy data.
- (decision) User selected Coaching path and has ample time. They are new to AI-agent workflows and want an explicit confirmation when the finished product brief is complete.
- (decision) Problem context: production plans are set about one week ahead but change with customer orders. Most critical risk is missing packaging such as cups or lids, which can halt production. Expiry is a secondary consideration. Supplier lead-time uncertainty matters; the product should record order-sent and delivery-received dates across many suppliers to make lead-time performance visible.
- (decision) Decision-support principle: show all relevant information without having the system decide what is critical. Use a priority list to guide attention, ordered as immediate shortage warning first, then recommended order quantity/latest safe order date, then supplier comparison. User controls whether to drill into recommendations or comparisons.
- (decision) Demo data approach approved: preloaded fictional data plus simple forms to change production plans and record supplier deliveries. User requested support creating coherent fictional dairy data later.
- (decision) Version-one success criteria: correctly recalculate ingredient and packaging requirements after a production-plan change; immediately reveal likely shortages; recommend order quantity and latest safe order date; show recorded supplier delivery performance; and have AI explain why each warning or recommendation appears.
- (decision) Longer-term vision: add demand forecasting, deeper production scheduling, and integration with a real ERP. These are future phases and explicitly outside version one.
- (decision) Add version-one descriptive analytics: show volume trends that are increasing or decreasing. This is historical/statistical visibility, not advanced demand forecasting. Version-one exclusions confirmed: real ERP/supplier integrations, automated supplier ordering, advanced forecasting, full production scheduling/capacity optimization, real company data, and enterprise-grade security.
- (decision) Analytics scope confirmed: production/demand volume trends by product, inventory movement or consumption by material, and supplier delivery performance including average lead time and late deliveries.
- (event) Brief finalized: all substantive memlog decisions are captured in brief.md; no addendum is needed because no downstream-only detail or rejected-alternative rationale was supplied. Structure and prose review completed; clarity and repetition edits applied.
- (override) Student template compliance correction: brief restructured to answer all seven required questions explicitly. Added What Makes This Different and replaced generic functional success criteria with measurable fictional-demo scenarios and a usability check.
- (decision) Refinement for student-template compliance: primary user outcome is avoiding production stoppages caused by missing materials or packaging, while enabling safer procurement decisions when production plans change. Why-now framing will use the established trigger: customer-order changes after weekly planning require rapid assessment of supplier lead-time and packaging risk.
- (event) Validated revised brief against BMAD_Product_Brief_Student_Template.pdf. All seven required questions and final quality-check dimensions are explicitly covered; brief is 820 words and remains within a plausible 1–2-page executive-brief range depending on final formatting.
- (decision) MRP v1 calculation contract approved: one-level finished-product-to-material BOM only; gross need is planned quantity times BOM quantity; net need is the positive remainder after usable on-hand inventory; and a positive net need is the recommended order quantity. Safety stock, minimum order quantities, order multiples, reservations, and open-order supply are outside this rule.
- (decision) Deadline policy approved: each supplier-material combination uses one fixed calendar-day lead time, and the latest safe order date is production date minus that lead time. Historical delivery performance is descriptive and never changes the recommendation quantity or deadline.
- (decision) The fixed fictional validation set is Blueberry Yogurt with blueberry puree, one cup, and one lid. It covers a plan increase, a cup shortage, and a delayed DairyPack AS lid delivery, with hand-calculated quantities and dates in calculation-rules.md.
- (decision) Alerts must expose their calculation basis and remain buyer decision support. Missing BOM, inventory, or lead-time input blocks the affected output rather than being assumed as zero; incomplete delivery dates are visibly marked and excluded from delivery statistics.
- (decision) AI explanations use OpenAI with server-side `OPENAI_MODEL=gpt-5.6-sol`. The real `OPENAI_API_KEY` belongs only in an ignored local environment file; the committed `.env.example` contains an empty key and the approved model name.
- (decision) Every alert immediately shows an English deterministic standard explanation. With complete valid server configuration, the buyer may explicitly choose **Generate AI explanation**; the model is never called automatically and no paid account is required for the core workflow.
- (decision) The calculation engine is the sole numerical and operational source of truth. The model receives only version-bound structured fictional facts and permitted options, returns no prose, and selects only closed server-owned templates and evidence bindings. Invalid, stale, unavailable, or timed-out responses fall back to the standard explanation.
- (decision) AI generation is server-only and cost-bounded through authenticated requests, deduplication, one active request per alert result, user rate and usage caps, and cancellation on timeout or disconnect.
